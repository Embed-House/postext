# Composed pages

**The template holds the form of the page; the Markdown holds its content and says which frame each piece goes to.**

A composed page is a page laid out from a template: named frames (a title band, a text, a box with its photo), groups that move together and a decoration, all defined in the configuration under `pageTemplates`. The Markdown of the page is a fenced block that names its template and pours content into the frames by name. No position or size is ever written in the Markdown, and no content text is ever written in the configuration, so a translation is the same template with other words.

This page covers the Markdown and how the page is set from it. The template itself (its frames, sizes, styles and decoration) is described in [Configuration › Composed pages](/en/docs/configuration-composed-pages). A document without composed pages lays out exactly as before.

## The page fence

```md
::::page{template="tema-cajas" fondo="banda-retos"}
# La actividad económica y el dinero

Las personas utilizan :chip[recursos]{style="clave"} que se obtienen de la naturaleza…
::::
```

A composed page opens with a line of **four colons** followed by `page` and its attributes, and closes with a bare line of the **same number of colons**. Inside, three-colon containers (`:::frame`, `:::callout`, `:::figure`, `:::columns`, `:::paper`) open and close as they always do, so a bare `:::` closes the innermost of them and never the page. A page written with five colons (`:::::page`) closes with five; a four-colon line inside it is then ordinary text.

A composed page always starts a new page (or spread), and the text after its closing fence starts on the next one.

The three-colon `:::page{split=…}` is a comic page (see [Comics](/en/docs/comics)) and keeps that meaning. A three-colon `:::page` that carries `template=` is still read as a comic page, and the warning `pageFenceLength` says that a composed page needs four colons.

When a fence is missing, the parser recovers without losing text:

- a page left open runs to the end of the chapter and is reported as an unclosed container at its opening line;
- a `::::page` line met while a page is open closes the open page there first (reported the same way);
- the closing fence closes any frame or box still open inside the page, reporting each one;
- a closing fence with another number of colons is not the page's: it stays in the text, where you can see it.

## Frames and slots

```md
:::frame{slot="dinero" label="El dinero"}
Es el instrumento utilizado como medio de pago…

Hasta hace pocos años, lo más normal era pagar con dinero en **efectivo**…
:::
```

A **slot** is the name a piece of content asks for; it matches the name of a frame of the template. `:::frame{slot="…"}` sends everything inside it to that frame. Its body is full Markdown: paragraphs, lists, headings, chips, `::resource` embeds, `:ref` references, footnotes, `:::columns` and nested callouts. `label` and `title` give the frame's tab or title its words, as they do for a `:::callout`; the frame's look comes from the callout style the template gives it.

How the content is shared out:

- Several pieces with the same slot are joined in Markdown order.
- Content with no slot goes to the template's **flow frame** (the frame marked `flow`).
- A heading with no slot goes first to the frame that `takes` its level (`takes: "h1"`), if the template has one, so the title of a textbook topic is just `# Title` at the top of the page.
- A `:::toc` with no slot goes to the frame that `takes: "toc"`, if the template has one (see *The contents in a frame*, below).
- Content that names a slot the template does not have goes to the flow frame and is reported (`slotUnknown`).

A `slot` outside a composed page is read and changes nothing.

## Figures with labels

```md
:::figure{resource="mapa-europa" slot="mapa"}
::label[OCÉANO ATLÁNTICO]{at="atlantico" style="mar"}
::label[**Madrid**]{at="madrid"}
::label[Mar Negro]{at="62% 48%"}
:::
```

`:::figure{resource="…"}` places one resource, with its caption and number, as `::resource` does, and lists the labels to set on the picture: one `::label[TEXT]{…}` line each, blank lines allowed between them.

- `at` names an anchor of the resource (a point defined once on the picture, shared by every language), or gives a point in percentages of the picture, `at="x% y%"`, for a label with no anchor.
- `style` names a label style and overrides the anchor's.
- The text in brackets is inline Markdown: bold, italic, chips.

A figure works in the running text as well as in a frame. Its labels are drawn on the picture as text and go wherever the figure goes: a column, a float, a frame turned or scaled by the reflow ladder, a spread. A line inside a figure that is neither a label nor blank ends a figure whose closing `:::` is missing, and is reported as an unclosed container.

### A figure's own words

The resource holds one picture, its anchors and one set of words, but each edition of a book says its own. A figure can give its `alt`, its `caption` and its `note` itself, in the fence or in lines of its own, and they replace the resource's for the pages of that file:

```md
:::figure{resource="relieve" alt="Mapa de relieve de Varenia"}
::caption[Relieve de **Varenia**, con sus tres cordilleras]
::note[Fuente: levantamiento de 1932]
::label[Mar de Sal]{at="mar"}
:::
```

- As fence attributes `alt`, `caption` and `note` take a short text between quotes. The `::alt[…]`, `::caption[…]` and `::note[…]` lines take the text in brackets, which may hold inline Markdown and `\\` for a line break, as a resource's caption and note do. A line wins over an attribute of the same name; lines go among the labels, with blank lines allowed.
- A word the figure does not give stays the resource's. An empty one says nothing: `caption=""` drops the caption, `alt=""` marks the picture as decorative.
- The words replace the resource's in the chapter that says them: the layout, the alternative text of every output and the tagged PDF read them, and the resource itself is unchanged. A resource has one set of words in a chapter, the first figure's: a later figure of the same resource that says other words is not shown them, and `figureWordsShared` says so. A reduced copy that needs a caption of its own takes a second resource that points at the same picture file. Unlike a label, a caption is not mapped character by character to the Markdown; `sourceAt` answers a click on it as it does for a resource's caption.

### How a label is set

- **Where.** A label stands at its anchor's point, a fraction of the whole picture, so a crop to the picture's safe area or a picture set smaller moves the label with the art. The anchor's `align` says which side of the label stands at the point, and `verticalAlign` which edge stands at its height. `start` and `end` follow the label's own direction: in Arabic, `start` is the label's right edge. `rotate` turns the label about the point, clockwise, at any angle: a river along its course, hills on a slant.
- **Look.** The label's own `style`, else its anchor's, names a label style (see Configuration › Composed pages); without one the label takes the body text's type. A halo strokes the letters under their fill; a plate (`background`) fills the box round the text, `padding` wider.
- **Fit.** With a `width` on its anchor, the label breaks between words within that measure, never inside a word. When a word is wider than the measure, the whole label is set smaller, down to its style's `minScale`; a label still too wide at that size is drawn with its widest word cut. Without a `width` the label keeps to one line, except where it breaks with `\\`, as in a caption.
- **Text.** The text in brackets is inline Markdown, set as a caption is: bold, italic, chips, highlight marks, references, inline formulas. A name of several words is one label, whatever the order of its words in each language: `::label[ATLANTIC OCEAN]` and `::label[OCÉANO ATLÁNTICO]` stand at the same anchor. Right-to-left labels are set right to left; Chinese and Japanese labels break between characters.
- **Outputs.** The canvas, the PDF, the HTML viewer, the fixed-layout EPUB and Folio draw the labels. On a right-to-left page a label stays where the picture shows it.
- **Vertical.** With `writingMode: 'vertical-rl'` on the anchor (or on its label style) the label is set as a vertical page is: its characters upright in cells one under another, the columns going from right to left, Latin words and long numbers turned sideways, short numbers in one cell. `width` is then a share of the picture's height, the length of a column, and a Chinese or Japanese label wraps into more columns. `align` runs along the column (`start` is its top), `verticalAlign` across the columns (`top` is the first, right-hand one), and `rotate` turns the label further. On the mirrored page of a right-to-left book it reads the same way, down its columns and upright, at the same place of the picture (since postext 1.40).
- **Checks.** A label still wider than its measure at its style's `minScale` raises `labelOverflow`; two labels whose lines meet raise `labelOverlap` on the later one. Both are checked in each edition, so a translation with longer words shows where it needs room. A part's palette (`:::part{palette="…"}`) recolours the labels on its pages as it does captions. The contrast check reads a label on its plate, else inside its halo; a label bare on the picture is skipped, since its ground is the picture. The print preflight counts a label's small process-colour type, its inks and its distance to the trim.

### labelAnchorUnknown

A `::label` names, with `at`, an anchor its figure's resource does not have. The label is not drawn; the reflowable EPUB still lists it and the picture's alternative text names it. The warning carries the resource, the anchor, the anchors the resource has (`known`), the locale, the page and the source range of the label's text. Correct the anchor id, add the anchor to the resource, or place the label with `at="x% y%"`.

In the Sandbox's Checks panel the warning leads to the label in the Text panel, and its pin button opens the picture's anchors with that name ready to place: one click on the picture adds the anchor.

### anchorUnlabelled

An anchor of the figure's resource names a label style, and no `::label` of the figure stands at it in this locale: the map has room for a word this edition leaves out. It is a note; `postext check` lists it as `info`. Anchors with no style, such as a comic speaker's, are not reported. The warning carries the resource, the anchor, its style, the locale, the page and the figure's source range. Write the label in this language's Markdown, or take the style off the anchor.

In the Checks panel it leads to the figure in the Text panel, and its pin button opens the picture's anchors on that anchor.

### labelOverflow

A label is still wider than its anchor's `width` at the smallest scale its label style allows (`minScale`): it is drawn at that scale and runs past its measure. The warning carries the resource, the label's text, its anchor (none for a label placed by percentages), the width it was given, the scale reached, the locale, the page and the source range of the label's text. It is checked in each edition, so a language with longer words overflows where another does not. Widen the anchor's `width`, shorten the word in this edition's Markdown, or lower the label style's `minScale`. A Chinese or Japanese label breaks between characters and does not overflow this way. Since postext 1.39.

### labelOverlap

The lines of two labels of one figure meet; a label with a plate counts as its plate. The later label, in the order written, carries the warning, with the text of the first earlier label it meets and how many earlier labels it meets in all. The test uses each line's own box, turned with the label, so a short line beside a long one leaves its side free, and lets two lines run into each other by up to an eighth of a line's height. It is checked in each edition. Move one of the anchors, narrow a label's `width` so that it breaks, or shorten the words in this edition's Markdown. In the Sandbox's Checks panel the pin button opens the picture's anchors at the label's anchor. Since postext 1.39. With `overlap: 'allow'` on an anchor, or in its label style, a label that the source prints touching its neighbours (a dense map) leaves the check, and no pair it belongs to is reported. Since postext 1.42. A picture's `avoid` zones count as the symbols of a map: rectangles drawn over its dots and icons in the **Avoid zones** mode of the picture's marks, in fractions of the picture. A label that runs onto one is reported the same way, naming `symbol 2` instead of a label (`withSymbol` in the data), and `overlap: 'allow'` leaves a label out of both checks. Since postext 1.42.

### figureWordsShared

A `:::figure` gives words (`alt`, `caption` or `note`) to a resource that an earlier figure of the same chapter has already worded, and they differ. The pages show the first figure's words and leave these out. The warning carries the resource, the words left out and the source range of the first of them; a later figure that gives no words of its own is no problem. Give the figure a resource of its own: a second resource that points at the same picture file, with its own caption, number and anchors. In the Sandbox's Checks panel it is filed under Figures. Since postext 1.42.

## Example

The page of a school textbook, with a title band, a text with its list and three coloured boxes, each with its photo beside it:

```md
::::page{template="tema-cajas" fondo="banda-retos"}
# La actividad económica y el dinero

## La actividad económica

Las personas utilizan :chip[recursos]{style="clave"} que se obtienen de la naturaleza…

- Las :chip[empresas.]{style="clave"} Ofrecen bienes y servicios a cambio de un beneficio.
- Las :chip[personas y familias.]{style="clave"} Adquieren lo que ofrecen las empresas…

:::frame{slot="dinero" label="El dinero"}
Es el instrumento utilizado como medio de pago, ya que su valor es conocido…
:::

::resource{id="dinero-pago" slot="dinero-fotos"}

:::frame{slot="gasto" label="El gasto"}
- **Fijo:** es el que se produce todos los meses…
- **Variable:** puede cambiar según las necesidades…
:::

::resource{id="gasto-compras" slot="gasto-fotos"}

:::frame{slot="ahorro" label="Préstamos y ahorro"}
Si necesitas o quieres comprar algo muy caro…
:::

::resource{id="ahorro-balanza" slot="ahorro-fotos"}
::::
```

The title goes to the frame that takes `h1`; the subheading, the paragraph and the list go to the flow frame; each box and each photo go to the frame of their slot. The Catalan edition is the same lines with other words: the template, the slots and the photos do not change.

## How the page is set

A composed page takes a page of its own (role `composed` for the `pages` filter of design elements). The template is chosen for the side the page falls on, so its `variants.recto` or `variants.verso` applies. Then each frame is placed and filled, one after another, with the typography of the rest of the book: paragraph and heading styles, lists, chips, footnote markers and the callout style of the frame.

### Placing the frames

A frame stands in its grid cell, or where its `placement` puts it (an anchor, an edge and an offset, as for a design element), or, with neither, at the top-left corner of the template's area, as wide as the area. A frame anchored to another one (`"to": "#dinero"`) is placed after it and moves with it: `below` hangs it under the other frame, `right-of` beside it, and a corner such as `top-right` pins it to that corner of the other frame's box.

Its height is fixed (a length or a percentage), `fill` (down to the edge of the area), or `auto`, the default: the height of the content it receives, between `minHeight` and `maxHeight`, and never past the foot of the area. A frame taller than its content places it as `verticalAlign` says (`top`, `middle`, `bottom`, or `justify` to spread the space between its blocks). The frame's callout style draws its background, border, rounded corners, stripe and icon at the frame's full size; the `label` of the `:::frame` fence prints in the style's tab.

### The flow frame and headings

Content that names no slot goes to the frame marked `flow`; the first heading of a level goes to the frame that `takes` it. The frames of a page are read in the order their content first appears in the Markdown, each one whole: a screen reader, the tagged PDF and the reflowable outputs read a box after the text that opens the page, not in the middle of it.

A heading set in a frame keeps the design of its level or heading style (a tab, a band, `{titleText}` set by the design), laid out at the frame's width as in a page column, and the frame keeps the room the design takes; a frame whose text is scaled scales the design with it, a turned frame turns it. See [In boxes and frames](/en/docs/configuration-headings#in-boxes-and-frames). Up to postext 1.36 such a heading was set plain.

### The contents in a frame

A frame can set the table of contents (since postext 1.42). Give the template a frame with `takes: "toc"` and write `:::toc` in the page fence, or write it inside the `:::frame` that names the slot:

```md
::::page{template="contents"}
# In this unit {toc="false"}

:::toc{keep="part" levels="1-2"}
::::
```

The frame sets the rows by the rules of the contents, as the running text does. A part has its row, drawn from `toc.parts.design`; an entry keeps its number, its leader and its page label, and links to the page it lists; the frame adds no paragraph spacing between entries. `keep` works in the frame: with `overflow.continueIn: "next-page"`, a block of the contents that a whole frame can hold goes whole to the added page, and a block taller than the frame is cut after its head and `toc.groups.minEntriesAfterHead` entries, the added page opening with the head repeated (`toc.groups.continuation`). The mark at the cut (`overflow.mark`) and the words at the head of the added page (`overflow.fromMark`) are those of any frame.

The title above the list is an ordinary heading, and the contents list it unless its heading style says `toc: false` or the heading says `{toc="false"}`, as in the running text. The ground of the page, a colour or a picture under the whole sheet, is the template's decoration: it paints under the frames and repeats on every page the contents add. A page break between parts (`toc.parts.breakBefore`) has no effect inside a frame.

### Chaining frames

`next` sends what a frame cannot hold to another frame of the same page, and that frame to another, like threaded text frames:

```json
{ "name": "columna-1", "flow": true, "next": "columna-2", "height": { "value": 120, "unit": "mm" } },
{ "name": "columna-2", "placement": { "anchor": { "to": "#columna-1", "edge": "right-of" } } }
```

The text is cut between lines and goes on in the next frame, broken again at that frame's width, with nothing lost or repeated. `overflow.continueIn: "#frame"` means the same.

A frame is cut as the running text is. A heading never ends a piece: it goes on with the lines it introduces. A cut between the items of a list leaves at least two items on each side (one when `bodyText.avoidOrphansInLists` or `bodyText.avoidWidowsInLists` is off), and under `bodyText.shortListSplit: 'avoid'` a list of up to `bodyText.shortListMaxItems` items is not cut between its items. When no cut allows that, the piece ends before the heading or the list, or inside one of the list's items. To go on on a page added after this one, see [When the text continues](#when-the-text-continues).

### Frames that push text

A frame with `exclude: "push"` keeps the text of the other frames out of its rectangle: a line of a frame that meets it is set short on the side the rectangle covers, as text wraps round a picture in the running text. A photo anchored to the corner of a box and marked `push` makes the box's text run beside it. A box that should keep a fixed column free for its pictures can use `padding` instead.

With `"wrap": "contour"` as well, the frame pushes with the shape of the pictures it holds rather than its rectangle: the alpha channel of a cut-out illustration, or the polygon in its resource's `contour`. Each line of the other frame is set as short as the shape is wide beside it, so the text of a box follows the outline of a drawing laid over it, with no line breaks or indents written line by line. Captions and other text in the pushing frame keep their boxes, a turned frame pushes with its box, and a picture whose shape is not known pushes with its box too, with a `textWrap` warning (reason `noContour`). See [Document format › Contour wrap](/en/docs/document-format-resources#contour-wrap) for the shape, its threshold and its margin.

```json
{ "name": "drawing", "exclude": "push", "wrap": "contour", "layer": 2,
  "placement": { "anchor": { "to": "#box", "edge": "top-right" } }, "width": { "value": 150, "unit": "mm" } }
```

### Decoration, running heads and layers

The template's `decoration` (pictures, rules, boxes and short texts) is laid once the frames have their final boxes, so an element can anchor to a frame (`"to": "#gasto"`) and follow it. Its texts read the page's own attributes: `fondo="banda-retos"` on the page fence gives `{attr.fondo}`, for example a background picture chosen page by page with `"resourceId": "{attr.fondo}"`. The running heads read them too. A template's `header` or `footer` replaces the section's, `false` hides it, and with neither the section's running heads print. On a right-to-left or vertical page the frames follow the mirrored or turned flow while the decoration stays on the sheet: an element placed at a start or end edge (`top-start`, see [Element placement](/en/docs/configuration-headers-footers#element-placement)) stands by its frame in every edition.

Frames paint in the order of their `layer`: the decoration at layer 0, frames at 1 by default, a higher layer over a lower one, a negative layer under the decoration. `rotate` turns a frame and its content together by any angle, about the frame's centre, in the canvas, the PDF, the HTML viewer and the fixed-layout EPUB.

### Content that does not fit

A frame whose content is taller than the frame, with no next frame to go on in and no page to continue on (`overflow.continueIn`), keeps all of it in the document but shows only what fits: the rest is cut at the frame's foot on paged output, and the warning [`frameOverflow`](#frameoverflow) says how much. Content that no frame takes (no flow frame, or a frame missing on this side) is set in the running text on the page after the composed page.

## Outside print: HTML, EPUB and tagged PDF

Every output reads a composed page in the order of its Markdown, not in the order the template lists its frames or paints its layers.

- **Reflowable EPUB.** The page becomes a section (`<section class="pt-composed pt-composed-tema-cajas">`) that opens with the page's page-break marker. Each frame is one block of it (`<div class="pt-frame pt-frame-dinero">`), placed where its content first appears in the Markdown; a frame and the frames its `next` chain goes on in make one block, and so does a frame whose content continues on added pages: the pieces are joined, a paragraph cut between two pages is one paragraph, and the added page's page-break marker stands inside it, where that page's text starts. A frame drawn in a callout style keeps that style's box: background, border, padding, body text, and its `label` as the box's title. A frame with no style is a plain block. The decoration (background pictures, cards, design text) belongs to the printed page and is left out.
- **Figure labels.** The HTML viewer and the fixed-layout EPUB write each drawn label as text right after its picture, in the order written (`<div class="pt-figure-label">`, turned by a CSS matrix), so a screen reader reads the picture's alternative text and then its words. The picture an EPUB carries does not show them. The reflowable book draws the plain ones over the picture as one SVG laid over the image (`pt-label-layer`, in the picture's own coordinates, so the words scale with it; since postext 1.40): words in bold or italic, the halo and the plate of their style, turned by `rotate`. A vertical label, and one with a formula, a chip or a mark, are left out of the SVG. The labels of a right-to-left book are drawn as they stand on the sheet (since postext 1.42): each word at the x the print measured, and a word of a right-to-left script as its own right-to-left text anchored at its right edge, so the browser joins its letters. All of them are listed after the picture, inside its figure: a list hidden from sight (`pt-figure-labels pt-hidden`) that a screen reader reads once. A label that is not drawn (its anchor is unknown) follows the picture's alternative text instead, `A map of Europe (Madrid)`; Chinese and Japanese labels take `（…、…）`, Arabic ones the Arabic comma.
- **HTML viewer and fixed-layout EPUB.** Both show the printed page with its template applied: each frame in its box, turned by `rotate`, clipped when its content overflows, painted over the frames of lower layers, the decoration at layer 0. The markup lists the frames in reading order and paints them in layer order with `z-index`, so assistive technology follows the Markdown while the page looks as printed. Decorative pictures are hidden from assistive technology (`alt=""`). The decoration's faces are among those the Sandbox embeds in an EPUB; a fixed-layout EPUB written without one of them reports `missingFont`.
- **Tagged PDF.** The structure tree reads the frames in the same order. Each frame, or chain of frames, is a `Div` holding its headings, paragraphs and lists, and the pieces of a frame continued on added pages are that same `Div` (a paragraph cut between pages is one `P`, the continuation mark an artifact); each of its pictures is a `Figure`, followed by a `Div` with one `P` per drawn label in the order written. A label's halo and plate are layout artifacts, so its words are read once; the decoration is a layout artifact too.

From code, a figure's labels are on its block as `resourceBlock.labels`: text, anchor or point, style and source range, plus `layout` (the lines, box, turn and look of a drawn label) and `rect` (where it is painted, which `sourceAt` answers). `composedReadingOrder(composedPaintOrder(page))` lists a page's decoration and frames in reading order.

## Warnings

The checks read the Markdown and the configuration before any layout, so they show in the Checks panel as you type, in `postext check` and in the document's `contentWarnings`, each with the source range of what it concerns (see [Configuration › Warnings in the document](/en/docs/configuration-programmatic-usage#warnings-in-the-document)).

### frameOverflow

This warning comes from the layout, not from the source checks above: it appears once the page has been set, in the Checks panel, in `postext check` and in the document's `contentWarnings`.

**Cause.** The content poured into a frame (through its slot, or as the flow frame's content) is taller than the frame, the frame has no `next` frame left to continue in, and none of the steps of the [reflow ladder](#when-it-still-does-not-fit) made it fit. Most often there is more text than the frame holds: a translation that runs longer than the original, or a frame held to a fixed `height` or `maxHeight`. What does not fit is cut at the foot of the frame in paged output; it stays in the document, in the source map and in the reflowable EPUB. The warning gives the template, the frame, the page, the document's `locale`, the height cut (`overflowMm`), `configPath` (the JSON pointer of the frame in the configuration, `/pageTemplates/0/frames/2`), the steps it tried (`stepsTried`), the scale its text and pictures were left at (`scale`, `imageScale`) and `proposals`, the changes that would make it fit.

**Fix.** Apply one of the proposals: each comes with its value, worked out on the page (see [The warning and its proposals](#the-warning-and-its-proposals)). Otherwise give the frame more room, chain it to another frame with `next`, shorten the text, or set the page with another template.

An empty frame is reported too. In a stack whose frames have nothing more to give, a frame cut below its `minHeight` or its fixed height (an answer space, a picture slot) overflows by the height cut, with no content at all. Its proposals lower its own `minHeight`, or the `minHeight` of a frame of its group that holds less content than that; they never take the room of another empty frame.

### frameContinuationLimit

**Cause.** A frame whose `overflow.continueIn` is `'next-page'` or `'template:<id>'` sent what it could not hold to an added page, and that page's frame sent the rest on again, page after page, until the composed page had added the most pages it may: 16 (see [When the text continues](#when-the-text-continues)). What is left is cut at the foot of the frame on the last added page; it stays in the document, the source map and the reflowable EPUB. The warning gives the template and frame of that page, the number of pages added (`pages`), the height cut (`overflowMm`), the frame's `configPath` and the page. Most often the frame that takes the content on the added page is far smaller than the text.

**Fix.** Give that frame more room (its height, or an `overflowTemplate` with a larger frame), or shorten the text. A box that runs over more than a few pages usually belongs in the running text.

### continueTargetMissing

**Cause.** A frame's `overflow.continueIn` is `'page:<id>'` (see [Jumping to a later page](#jumping-to-a-later-page)), and no page fence `::::page{id="<id>"}` comes after the page the story is on: the id is misspelt, the fence lacks it, the fence attribute the rule reads (`'page:{attr.sigue}'`) is missing, or the fence comes before (`before: true`). The rest goes on on a page added right after, as with `'next-page'`. The warning gives the template, the frame, `target` (the id it looked for), the frame's `configPath` and the page. The id may also belong to a page fence of a later chapter of the book; a chapter laid out on its own with `buildDocument` and no `laterPageIds` knows only its own pages.

**Fix.** Give the later page fence that id, or correct the name in `continueIn` or in the fence attribute it reads. A page before the story cannot take it: move that fence after the story, or name a later page.

## When the text grows

A composed page rearranges itself inside the limits its template declares. Write a longer text in the first box of a page whose boxes form a stack group, and the box grows to hold it while the boxes below, and the photos and decoration anchored to them, move down; no line of the template changes. A shorter text gives the box back its declared height. A frame or a decoration element marked `pin` stays where the template puts it, for a box that has to sit on the artwork. How stacks and rows share space, and which frame gives way first, is set in the template: see [Configuration › How groups share space](/en/docs/configuration-composed-pages#how-groups-share-space).

When the boxes reach the foot of the area and no frame of the group can give more space, the frame that does not fit climbs the reflow ladder below; groups add no warning of their own.

## When it still does not fit

A frame whose content does not fit tries, one after another, the ways its template allows to make room, and stops at the first that works. Each step is tried on top of the ones before it, in this order:

  <thead>

    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><code>redistribute</code></td>
      <td>The other frames of its group give space back whether they <code>shrink</code> or not: each goes down to its <code>minHeight</code>, never below what its own content needs. In a row, they give width, each down to the narrowest width its own text still fits in.</td>
    </tr>

    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><code>continue</code></td>
      <td>The frame continues on another page (<code>overflow.continueIn: 'next-page'</code>, <code>'template:&lt;id&gt;'</code> or <code>'page:&lt;id&gt;'</code>): it is cut at its foot with its mark and the rest goes on on a page added after this one (see <a href="#when-the-text-continues">When the text continues</a>). A frame that can continue on another page takes that step earlier: after `redistribute`, before `scaleImages` and `shrinkText`. A story set over several pages keeps one type size, and its last page is not set smaller than the pages before it. Its pictures and text shrink only when it cannot go on: on the last page continuation may add, or when the frame that would take the rest takes none of it. `fit.preferContinue: false` keeps the order of the table, for a box that should lose a few points rather than add a page.

When no step makes the content fit, the frame is cut at its foot, keeps the scales the ladder reached, and the layout reports `frameOverflow`. A frame on an added page that takes none of the content it receives does not send it on again (it would add pages for ever): it reports `frameOverflow` too, with `continue` among the steps tried. Each step lays the whole page out again, so the result depends only on that page: the same input gives the same page, frames that overflow are taken in template order, and a change on one composed page leaves every other page as it was. The settings are in [Configuration › When content does not fit](/en/docs/configuration-composed-pages#when-content-does-not-fit).

## When the text continues

A frame can send what it cannot hold to a page added after its composed page, or to a later page of the book. The rule is the frame's `overflow.continueIn`:

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th style={{ width: '200px' }}>Value</th>
      <th>Where the content goes on</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td><code>'#frame'</code></td>
      <td>Another frame of the same page, as <code>next</code> (see <a href="#chaining-frames">Chaining frames</a>).</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><code>'next-page'</code></td>
      <td>The same frame on a page added after this one, built from the template's <code>overflowTemplate</code>. Without one, the page is the same template laid again with only that frame filled: the other frames take no room (their heights follow their empty content and they leave their groups), so the frame starts near the head of the page, and the decoration and running heads are the template's.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><code>'template:&lt;id&gt;'</code></td>
      <td>A page of that template.</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td><code>'page:&lt;id&gt;'</code></td>
      <td>A frame of the later page fence <code>::::page&#123;id="&lt;id&gt;"&#125;</code>, past the pages between (see <a href="#jumping-to-a-later-page">Jumping to a later page</a>).</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>

```json
{ "id": "tema-cajas", "overflowTemplate": "tema-sigue",
  "frames": [
    { "name": "dinero", "style": "caja-azul", "maxHeight": { "value": 40, "unit": "mm" },
      "overflow": { "continueIn": "next-page" } } ] },
{ "id": "tema-sigue",
  "frames": [ { "name": "dinero", "style": "caja-azul" } ] }
```

Continuing is a step of the [reflow ladder](#when-it-still-does-not-fit): a frame first grows and makes room in its group, and when that is not enough it is cut at its foot, before its pictures or its text are set smaller (unless its `fit.preferContinue` is `false`). Its text and pictures stay at full size, the piece ends with the continuation mark, and the rest goes on, broken again at the width of the frame that takes it, from the line where it was cut. Nothing is lost or repeated, and no warning is raised.

- **Which frame takes it.** On the added page the content goes on in the frame of the same name; when the template has none, in its flow frame; failing that, in its first frame not already taken. A story threaded through frames with `next` goes on where its chain starts: the rest of the third of three chained columns goes on in the first column of the added page (the frame of the chain head's name, else the flow frame), so a body template that continues into itself fills every column of every page. Several frames of a page that continue to the same template share one added page when that template has a frame for each; otherwise the next one gets a page of its own. A frame of the added page that receives nothing is left empty.
- **Again and again.** A piece that still does not fit goes on again, on the next added page, with the frame's own `overflow.continueIn` when it has one there, or else with the rule that brought it. A composed page adds at most 16 pages one after another; what still does not fit on the last one is cut and reported as [`frameContinuationLimit`](#framecontinuationlimit). A frame of an added page too small to take any of it reports `frameOverflow` instead of adding pages.
- **Where the piece is cut.** As on the page: a heading stays with its first lines and a list keeps two items on each side (see [Chaining frames](#chaining-frames)). When none of the box fits on the page, the whole box goes on, tab and all: the page is not left with a tab and a mark over an empty piece. It takes no room on the page it leaves, and when the answer space under it would not keep its size there, the box goes on whole with it.
- **What the pages keep.** The frame that is cut gives up its group's room first, so the frames after it keep their size. When the rest goes on on the same template laid again, the empty frames right after it in its stack (an answer space under an activity box: frames that receive no content) go with it. On the page they take no room; on the added page they keep their size under the rest of the box, with the decoration anchored to them. The other frames of that added page take no room and their decoration is not drawn, and a stack keeps the place its first frame has in the template, so the rest of the box starts where the stack starts, clear of the page's kicker and running head.
- **Ordinary pages.** The added pages are pages of the book: they are numbered, carry the running heads of their template (or the section's), take the `variants` of their side, and the `{attr.x}` of the page fence still reaches their decoration. An overflow template with `spread: true` opens on the next verso, with a parity blank before it when needed, and takes the recto after it; a spread whose `overflowTemplate` is a single page continues on single pages. The text after the page fence starts on the page after the last added page.
- **Ids.** A frame of the n-th added page is `cp0:<name>~<n>`; the frame that takes the content is named after the frame of the fence's own page that first sent it on (`cp0:dinero~1`, `cp0:dinero~2`), so a selection keeps its element whichever frame takes it. Each frame records the frame it continues from or into (`continuedFrom`, `continuesTo`), the page's `VDTPage.composed.continuationOf` is the index of the fence's own page, and the blocks of the pieces carry the box's id with `-cont-<n>`, as split callouts do.
- **Relayout.** A composed page whose content continues is laid out afresh, with its added pages, on every build: the page memo keeps only pages whose content stays on them.

### Jumping to a later page

A magazine story that outruns its pages jumps to a page further on, past other stories: «continued on page 47». `overflow.continueIn: 'page:<id>'` sends the rest to the page fence with that `id`, wherever it comes later in the chapter:

```markdown
::::page{template="articulo" sigue="p47"}
…
::::

::::page{template="otra"}
…
::::

::::page{template="saltos" id="p47"}
…
::::
```

```json
{ "name": "cuerpo", "flow": true,
  "overflow": { "continueIn": "page:{attr.sigue}",
    "mark": "Continued on page {page}", "fromMark": "Continued from page {page}" } }
```

- **Which page.** `<id>` may be `{attr.name}`, read from the page fence the story is on, so one article template serves every article and the Markdown says where each one jumps.
- **Which frame.** The frame `'page:<id>#<frame>'` names; without one, the frame of the sending chain head's name, else the first frame that page's own content leaves empty. The page keeps its own content in its other frames.
- **After the jump.** What that frame cannot hold goes on on pages added after the jump page, as `'next-page'` does from there; a story that finds no free frame on it goes on there too.
- **Ids and links.** The frames of the jump page keep their own ids (`cp2:resto`). The sending frame's `continuesTo` and the receiving frame's `continuedFrom` link the pieces, and the sending page's reflow log says «cuerpo continued on page 47». The reflowable EPUB and the tagged PDF read the story in one piece.
- **A page that does not follow.** An id that no later page fence has raises [`continueTargetMissing`](#continuetargetmissing), and the rest goes on on the next page.
- **In a later chapter file.** A magazine kept as one chapter file per article jumps the same way: the page with the `id` may be in a later chapter of the book (since postext 1.41). The sending chapter hands the rest over, and the chapter that has the page sets it in the frame the page leaves free, as it sets a jump inside one file. Footnotes, heading numbers, figure numbers and references in the rest keep the numbers their own chapter gave them, and the marks print the page numbers of the book: «Continued on page 47» names the page as the chapter that has it printed it, whatever the chapters between add. A click on the carried words puts the caret in the Markdown of the chapter they were written in. Several stories sent to one page take its free frames in the order of the chapters. Editing the article lays out the chapter with the page again; editing a chapter in between does not. The tagged PDF reads the rest on its page, after what the page holds itself, and the reflowable EPUB in the chapter that has the page. The rest is set in the typography of the book's text, not in that of a styled section of the chapter that has the page, and an index mark or an anchor written in it is listed on the page where its story starts.

The mark is set under the piece's last line, in the box's body face, aligned and slanted as the style's `continuesMarkerAlign` and `continuesMarkerItalic` say (right and italic by default). It is pagination furniture: the reflowable EPUB leaves it out, the tagged PDF marks it as an artifact. A frame chained with `next` on the same page carries no mark.

The piece on the other page opens without the frame's tab and title, as the later parts of a split box do. `overflow.fromMark` sets words at its head, `{page}` being the number of the page it comes from: `"Continued from page {page}"`. The receiving frame's own `fromMark` wins over the sending frame's; with neither set, the piece opens with no words. A style with `repeatTitle: true` repeats its title there with its `continuedSuffix` («(cont.)»). Both marks are set in the box's body face, aligned and slanted as the end mark is, and are pagination furniture.

## Reflow log

Each composed page keeps a log of what the layout changed against the template's own drawing: which frame grew, gave space back or moved, by how much, and which frame set it off. A page whose text fits its template logs nothing. Give it the height it is drawn at as `minHeight` to have both logged. The Cookbook's stress test (C38) compares such frames with the run on the text as written instead.

Changes under half a millimetre are left out, and each change is rounded once to a tenth of a millimetre, so frames that moved together read the same. Decoration that follows a frame is not listed.

`postext check` prints one `reflow` line per composed page that has events, after the warnings; with `--json`, the report holds a `reflow` array with one entry per page: `pageIndex`, `pageLabel`, `template`, `fenceIndex`, `chapter`, `events` and `lines` (the events in words).

From code, the log is `VDTPage.composed.log`, and each frame keeps the box its template declares in `VDTFrame.declaredRect` when it differs from `rect`. `composedReflowLog(doc)` returns the entries `postext check` prints, and `describeReflowEvent(event)` puts one event in words; `describeFitStep(step)` and `describeFrameOverflowProposal(proposal)` do the same for a step of the ladder and a proposal. A frame whose text the ladder scaled has `VDTFrame.scale` below 1: its blocks are laid out in a box `1 / scale` as large (`frameContentRect(frame)`) and `frameMatrix(frame)` puts them on the sheet; its pictures' scale is `VDTFrame.imageScale`.

## Spreads

A template with `spread: true` is one sheet of two facing pages: a band running across the spine, a picture strip from one outer edge to the other, cards on either side. The page fence says so with `spread`:

```md
::::page{template="linea-tiempo" spread}
# How we measure time

To know when to eat, work or rest, people have always needed to measure time…

:::frame{slot="banda-2"}
With machine clocks
:::

::resource{id="tira-tiempo" slot="tira"}
::::
```

- **Where it opens.** A spread starts on a verso, the left page of a left-to-right book and the right page of a right-to-left one, and takes the recto after it. When the text before it ends on a verso, the page after that is left blank (a parity blank, as before a comic spread), so the two halves face each other. The text after the closing fence starts on the page after the recto.
- **One sheet.** The frames are placed on a sheet as wide as both pages. The `content` area runs from the outer margin of one page to the outer margin of the other, with both inner margins inside it; `trim` is the two trimmed pages side by side; `bleed` adds the bleed at the head, the foot and both outer edges, never at the spine. A `grid` divides the whole width: six columns put three on each page. In a right-to-left book the sheet is mirrored as its pages are, so the first column stands on the right page.
- **The gutter.** `gutter` is a band of that width centred on the spine. A frame without `crossGutter: true` is kept off it: a frame reaching across the whole band is cut at the band's edge on the side of its centre, and one whose inner edge falls inside the band moves out of it whole. An `auto` height is measured at the narrower width, and the page's reflow log records the change (`resized` or `moved`, with cause `gutter`). Without a `gutter` the band is the spine itself, so only frames that ask for it cross the fold.
- **Across the spine.** A frame with `crossGutter: true` may run across. Its text is read once, on the page that holds the frame's centre; the facing page paints its own half of the frame, cut at its sheet, so a band or a picture reads as one piece across the open book. A tagged PDF marks that half as an artifact, the HTML hides it from assistive technology, and selecting or searching the text finds it once.
- **Decoration** is laid once over the whole sheet and cut at the spine the same way: a ground, a card from one page to the other, a running head on the right page. An element anchored to `#frame` follows that frame onto the page it lands on. A spread takes no `variants`, since it has both sides; `header` and `footer` work on each of its pages as on any page.

On the canvas, in the Sandbox's spread view and in the Folio the two pages stand side by side. The PDF has two pages, each clipped at its bleed. The fixed-layout EPUB gives them `page-spread-left` and `page-spread-right` with `rendition:spread-both`, so a reader shows them together in either orientation. The reflowable EPUB reads the frames as it reads any composed page, in Markdown order.

### spreadMismatch

**Cause.** The page fence and its template disagree: the fence has `spread` and the template is a single page, or the template is a spread (by its own `spread: true` or through `extends`) and the fence lacks the flag. The template decides: a spread template is laid over two facing pages, any other on one page. The warning gives the template, the fence's flag (`fence`) and the template's (`templateSpread`), and points at the `spread` attribute, or at the template's name when the fence has none.

**Fix.** Make the fence say what the template is: write `spread` on the fence of a spread template, remove it from the fence of a single-page one, or choose another template.

## Migrating a book with text in its styles

A book made before composed pages may hold the text of its pages in heading styles: a hidden heading on each page whose running head places every text where the printed page had it. The *Text held in the configuration* check lists it. In the Sandbox, **Move the text to the Markdown** in the Checks panel turns each page design into a page template whose frames stand where the texts stood, and each page into a page fence with its text in `:::frame` slots; it shows every page before and after, and can be undone. See [Moving the text to the Markdown](/en/docs/sandbox-composed-pages#moving-the-text-to-the-markdown). From code, `moveTextToMarkdown({ config, chapters, metadata }, options)` does the same and returns the new configuration, the chapters and a report.

With `labelsOnPictures: true` (since postext 1.38), the words a design placed on a picture become the picture's labels instead of frames: a text of one line and at most four words, no larger than three quarters of the body text (`labelsOnPictures: { maxFontSize }` sets another limit), whose box lies inside a picture of the same design. The picture leaves the decoration for a frame at the box it was drawn in, holding a `:::figure` with one `::label` line per text, and each text becomes an anchor of the picture's resource at the point where the design set it (its first baseline, the side its alignment names, its box's width), with a label style for its look. Pass the book's `resources` in the input: the result returns them with the anchors (`result.resources`), and a bitmap smaller than the box the design stretched it over gets the `resolution` that sets it at that width. A picture shown on several pages shares its anchors. The report's `labels` counts the pictures, labels, anchors and label styles. The Sandbox's dialog offers it as an option, off until asked for, when the book has words to turn into labels. When the design painted decoration over the picture (a tag across the map) and none under it, the picture's frame goes on layer `-1`, below the decoration, so the tag stays over the map (since postext 1.40). With decoration under the picture as well, or over one of its labels, a single slot cannot hold both orders: the frame stays on layer 0, and the page is reported as one that may look different (`overPicture`).

## From code

`composedPages(blocks)` reads the parsed blocks of a chapter (`parseMarkdownWithIssues(markdown).blocks`) and returns one node per page fence, in order: its `template`, `spread`, `attrs` with the source range of each value (`attrSources`), its `fenceIndex` (the ordinal of the fence in the chapter, the same in every language), `slots` (the indices of the blocks of each slot, in Markdown order), `unassigned` (the blocks with no slot) and the source range of the whole page. A block carries the slot it names in `ContentBlock.slot`, with the source range of the value; a figure is a `resourceBlock` whose `figure` holds its attributes and labels, each label with its text, its inline spans and a source map back to the Markdown. `checkComposedSlots(pages, config)` returns the three slot warnings for those pages.

After layout, a composed page's `VDTPage.composed` records its template, the variant applied, the page fence's attributes and, for each frame, its id (`cp0:dinero`: the page fence's ordinal and the frame's name, the same in every language), its box, layer, turn, the height of content that did not fit (`overflowPx`), the frame it continues from or into, its config path (`/pageTemplates/0/frames/2`) and the ids of its blocks. Those blocks are ordinary callout blocks in `page.floats` and `doc.blocks`, with their source ranges, so a click in an upright frame of the Sandbox preview lands on its Markdown. `composedPaintOrder(page)` gives the order a renderer paints the frames and the decoration in, and `frameMatrix(frame)` the turn of a frame.

## Source map

Everything a composed page paints knows where it was written, so an editor can go from a point of the page to the Markdown or the configuration, and from a range of the Markdown to what it became on the page.

`sourceAt(doc, pageIndex, x, y)` takes a point of a laid-out page, in page pixels from the sheet's top-left corner, and returns what is there, innermost first, each element followed by the ones that hold it. A glyph of a box comes with its line, its paragraph, its frame, the frame's groups and the template; a word of the decoration comes with its design element, the decoration and the template. Each element has a `level`, a stable `id`, a `source`, and, for most, its `rect` on the page (upright, before a turned frame's `rotate`).

On a right-to-left or vertical page the frames are laid in the page's flow frame, mirrored or turned a quarter on the sheet, while the decoration and the running heads stay on the sheet. `sourceAt` still takes sheet points and maps them for the frames. An element whose `rect` is in the flow frame comes with `flow: true`, in `sourceAt`, `elementsForSource` and `findElement` alike; `flowRectToPage(page, rect)` gives its box on the sheet, and `flowToPage(page, x, y)` a point of it. Up to postext 1.38 a sheet point on a frame of such a page found only the template.

The levels, innermost first: `text`, `attr`, `label`, `resource`, `line`, `block`, `box`, `frame`, `group`, `design`, `slot`, `template`. A frame and the template also carry `content`, the range of the Markdown they hold: the frame's slot content, the whole page fence. `file` is the name the host passes in the options (`{ file: 'tema-1.md' }`, or a function of the page index for a document stitched from several chapters).

```ts
import { buildDocument, sourceAt, elementsForSource, findElement } from 'postext';

const doc = buildDocument({ markdown, resources }, config);
const chain = sourceAt(doc, 0, 640, 1180, { file: 'tema-1.md' });
// [ { level: 'text', id: 'cp0:dinero-c0@12', source: { kind: 'markdown', start: 677, end: 678, file: 'tema-1.md' } },
//   { level: 'line', … }, { level: 'block', id: 'cp0:dinero-c0', … },
//   { level: 'frame', id: 'cp0:dinero', source: { kind: 'config', path: '/pageTemplates/0/frames/2' }, content: { start: 665, end: 942 } },
//   { level: 'template', id: 'cp0', source: { kind: 'config', path: '/pageTemplates/0' } } ]

elementsForSource(doc, 677, 690); // lines, blocks, frames, pages and design text the range touches
findElement(doc, 'cp0:dinero');   // the same frame after a relayout, with its rect
```

`elementsForSource(doc, start, end)` goes the other way and returns `{ pageIndex, element }` for every line, block, frame, composed page and design text the range touches. A line set past its frame's foot comes with `clipped: true`: it is cut on the page and kept in the source map. On a spread, the half of a frame that crosses the spine and is read on the facing page answers with the ids of the blocks it shows and `facing: true`, in `sourceAt` and in `elementsForSource`. There `elementsForSource` gives each page the frame with the part of its box that page paints as its `rect`, so a band that starts near the spine has a point to click on both pages. A figure label answers at level `label`, with its id and its anchor, wherever it is drawn on the picture.

**Stable ids.** A composed page is `cp<n>`, `n` the ordinal of its page fence in the chapter; a frame is `cp<n>:<frame>` (with `~<k>` for the k-th piece of its content that goes on on a later page), its blocks `cp<n>:<frame>-c0`, `-c1`…, a group `cp<n>:group:<name>`, an element of the decoration or the running heads `cp<n>:decoration:<id>`, `cp<n>:header:<id>`, `cp<n>:footer:<id>`, and a label of a figure `cp<n>:<slot>:label:<anchor>`. None of them depends on positions or on the words, so an id is the same after any relayout and in every language of the chapter: a selection survives an edit, and the same frame can be found in the translation. `findElement(doc, id)` finds the element again.

**Contents entries.** A row of `:::toc` carries in `tocEntry.headingSource` the offset of the heading it lists in its chapter's Markdown body (front matter not counted, as outlines count); `sourceAt` gives it as `heading` on the row's block.

**Relayout of one page.** The layout of each composed page is kept by what it reads: the template, the Markdown of the page, the styles and fonts, the language, the side of the sheet and its geometry. A later build that meets the same page (the next pass of the layout, an edit elsewhere in the chapter, the chapter paginated again in the same worker) takes it as it was. An edit inside a page, or a change of an attribute of its fence, sets that page alone; a change of the template sets every page that uses it. A page holding footnotes, references, citations or a contents listing is set afresh every time, since what it prints depends on the rest of the book. A spread is laid out afresh every time too: its two pages are one sheet. `VDTComposedPage.layoutKey` is the key of a page's frames; `composedPagePaintKey(page)` adds what is laid round them (running heads, decoration, page number), so a host can keep the pixels it painted while the key stays the same. The Sandbox canvas does: an edit on one page repaints that page.

## Comic pages

A comic page (`:::page{split}`) is a composed page whose frames its split generates: one per panel, with stable ids `cm<n>:<name>`, a polygon for a slanted cell, a callout style through `box`, and Markdown poured into a text panel. See [Comics › Comic pages and composed pages](/en/docs/comics#comic-pages-and-composed-pages).
