# Where a river is born

Rain and snow fall on the mountains. Some of the water runs down the slopes and some soaks into the ground. Underground it moves slowly, until it meets rock it cannot cross. Then it comes out into the open. A place where water comes out of the ground is called a **spring**.

## From spring to stream

A spring gives only a trickle. The trickle runs downhill and meets other trickles, and together they make a **stream**. On a steep slope a stream runs fast. It rolls pebbles along and wears them smooth. When streams meet, they make a bigger one. The River Aldara begins like this, in the snow of the White Range, 1,850 metres above the sea.

:::frame{slot="probar" label="Try it"}
Hold a wet sponge over a tray and squeeze it gently. Where does the water come out? Watch how it drips and then runs. That is how a spring feeds a stream.
:::

:::frame{slot="sabias" label="Did you know?"}
At its spring, the Aldara is a trickle you could step over. After 94 kilometres it is as wide as a football pitch is long.
:::

:::frame{slot="palabras" label="Words to learn"}
**Spring:** a place where water comes out of the ground.

**Stream:** a small river that runs fast down a slope.

**Tributary:** a stream that flows into a bigger river.

**Bank:** the land along the side of a river.
:::

# From the mountains to the sea

The River Aldara is 94 kilometres long. It starts in the White Range, crosses a valley and a plain, and reaches the sea at Aldamar. Geographers split a river into three parts. Each part is called a **course**, and each one looks different.

:::

:::frame{slot="curso" label="Middle course"}
The valley is wider and the river slows down. Farmers water their fields, and Molinar grew around a bridge and a mill.
:::

:::frame{slot="curso" label="Lower course"}
The land is almost flat. The river is wide and slow, and it bends in great loops. At Aldamar it flows into the sea.

# People and the river

People have always settled near rivers. They need water to drink, to wash and to grow food. A river also gives fish, a road for boats and the push that turns a wheel.

## Looking after the river

A river stays clean only if we keep it clean. Rubbish, oil and dirty water make the river sick, and the fish and plants that live in it suffer. Everything that goes down a drain or a street ends up in a river or in the sea.

:::frame{slot="cuidar" label="We look after our river"}
- We put rubbish in the bin, never in the water.
- We use water carefully and turn off the tap.
- We keep to the paths and leave plants and animals alone.
- We help to clean the bank when the school organises it.
:::

:::frame{slot="uso" label="1 · Water for the fields"}
In the middle course, farmers open small canals so the river water runs between the rows. Without it, tomatoes and lettuces would not grow in summer. A single canal can carry water for many kilometres.

:::figure{resource="riego" slot="uso-foto"}

:::frame{slot="uso" label="2 · Mills"}
Long ago the falling water turned the wheel of the mill at Molinar. The wheel moved the stones that ground wheat into flour for the whole valley. Today the old mill is a small museum.
