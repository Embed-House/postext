## What you'll build

Nine pages of *Our river*, unit 4 of an invented Year 3 social science book, on a 210 × 280 mm page, in Spanish, Catalan and English. A painted valley opens the unit, a contents page lists two parts, and three topics follow as double pages, each on its own ground: water blue, field green, sun sand. Every topic has a tab with its number and title, text in two columns and boxes with tabs that grow with their text. The second topic is one painted map across both pages, with the river, the villages and the three courses named on it from the Markdown. The three texts differ in length by a fifth or more and settle in the same templates: a box takes the height its words need and pushes the next one down, and the map is set a little smaller before any text is.

## Method

### 1 · Build the boxes with `extends`

<Excerpt region="boxes" />

A box style is made in three steps. `caja` holds the shape, the type and the geometry of the tab; `caja-tema` puts the topic's colour on the tab and the bullets; `dato`, `palabras` and `actividad` set the kind: a tinted box, an outlined one or one with a stripe. Their colours are palette entries (`tema`, `tema-tint`), so each style is written once and a topic's template tints it.

### 2 · Let every topic extend one base template

<Excerpt region="answer" />

The base is a spread: a ground to the bleed, a title tab with its number, and a text in two columns. A topic extends it (`extends: 'tema'`), sets its `palette` (the three `tema` entries take the family's colours, and the ground, the tab, the number and every box change together) and adds its own frames. The tab and the number are decoration anchored to the title frame, so the tab is as tall as the title the language needs. The number is `{headingNumber}`, the counter of the heading the frame took, painted over the frame's left padding: no page fence repeats it, and a click on it reaches the number, not the frame.

### 3 · Let the groups share the room

Each topic has two groups. The left page is a stack: title, text, then the strip of pictures or the map. The right page is another stack of boxes, or a row of three boxes that share its width. A frame is as tall as its words, never less than its `minHeight`, so the Spanish text pushes everything under it down and no position in the template changes. When a page runs short, the [reflow ladder](/en/docs/composed-pages#when-it-still-does-not-fit) takes space back, then sets the map and the pictures smaller, and the text last, down to 90 %. `pnpm cookbook bench` runs the three editions again with the text 30 % longer.

### 4 · Put the names on the map from the text

<Excerpt region="art-labels" />

The map is a painting with no letter on it. Each place is an anchor of the picture, a point in fractions of its width and height with a label style, and each edition writes its own `::label` lines at those anchors. *Penalta Reservoir*, *Pantà de Penyalta* and *Embalse de Peñalta* stand at the same place, and a longer name breaks inside the anchor's `width`.

### 5 · Keep each part of the contents together

<Excerpt region="contents" />

`:::toc{keep="part" levels="1-2"}` lists the topics and their sections and keeps each part row with its entries. A `:::part` fence with `parts.page: false` is only a row of the contents and a section for the running heads: it adds no divider page. The contents are a composed page: its template has a frame with `takes: "toc"` that receives the `:::toc` and lays it by the contents' own rules, so the part rows, the leaders and `keep` work as in the running text, and the pale ground is the template's decoration.

## Variations

### Add a fourth family

A topic about the sea is a fourth entry in `FAMILIES` and a template made with `topic('tema-mar', 'mar', { frames })`. `swap('mar')` writes its palette, and the boxes and the ground follow it.

```diff
-  sol: { main: '#9a5209', tint: '#fcefd2', ground: '#f8e6b8' },
+  sol: { main: '#9a5209', tint: '#fcefd2', ground: '#f8e6b8' },
+  mar: { main: '#0f5a6b', tint: '#dcf0f2', ground: '#cbe6ea' },
```

### Keep the text at its size

A frame that must not shrink its text takes `fit: { shrinkText: false }`. A longer text then pushes harder on the frames around it, and the ladder reaches the pictures first.

```diff
-  frames: [title, { name: 'texto', flow: true, columns: 2, columnGap: mm(7), width: mm(HALF),
-    fit: { minScale: 0.9 } }],
+  frames: [title, { name: 'texto', flow: true, columns: 2, columnGap: mm(7), width: mm(HALF),
+    fit: { shrinkText: false } }],
```

## Pitfalls

- A name painted into a picture could not change with the language: the map carries no word, and every name is a label.
A topic's palette changes the `tema` entries only. The folios and the contents use `agua`, which has the same blue, so they stay blue on the green and sand pages; link them to `tema` and they follow the topic.
- Two figures that set the same resource take the words of the first one: the small map on the contents page is the same file under its own resource, `rio`, with no anchors.
