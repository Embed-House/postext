## What you'll build

Twelve pages of a first-year handwriting book on A4, in ten editions that are ten different books. Each language gets the joined script its schools teach, from Google's Playwrite families (GB J, ES, FR Trad, DE VA, IT Trad, BR) or Playpen Sans Arabic, on the paper its children write on: the Spanish *pauta*, French Seyès squares with a red margin, the German *Lineatur* with its yellow middle band, Brazilian *caligrafia* lines. The Chinese edition writes in 田字格 squares, where children trace pale red characters (描红), with pinyin on four lines; the Japanese one in マス squares with a dotted cross, tracing in grey (なぞり書き). The lessons follow each spelling: ñ and the silent h in Spanish, l·l in Catalan, ß and capital nouns in German, the particles は, を and へ in Japanese, the four forms of a letter and the letters that do not join in Arabic. Every row goes from a model to dots to nothing. Twenty-nine paintings, generated with a diffusion model, are shared by all editions.

## Method

### 1 · Give each edition its school script and its paper

<Excerpt region="editions" />

`u` is the x-height in millimetres, the unit every ruling is drawn in: 3.6 mm for most editions, 3 mm for French, whose Seyès lines are one x-height apart. The Playwrite faces all draw their x-height at half the em, so the model's size is `u / 0.5`, and the ruling's lines fall where the face's own proportions put its ascenders and descenders. The Chinese text is set in Song (Noto Serif SC): LXGW WenKai TC centres its commas the way Hong Kong and Taiwan print them, so the Kai face sets only the titles and the squares.

### 2 · Set the model on the ruling, live

<Excerpt region="answer" />

A practice row is a box, and the word to practise is its content: `:::callout{type="palabra" title="Trace and write"}`, then `owl` on a line of its own, then `:::`. All the rows of one shape share a box style and differ only in their words. The style's `design` draws one picture, the ruling with the dotted copies, and sets the word on it as text in the school face through `{boxText}`; `setsContent` keeps the box from setting its content a second time under the drawing. The picture is named after the word (`trazo-{boxText}`), and the pen draws one for each word it finds in the boxes. A design text's baseline falls at 0.8 of its line height below the top of its box, so the model's box is placed at the row's baseline minus 0.8 of its line, and the letters stand on the line the ruling draws. The line is ten x-heights tall so that the box's clip falls 2 u below the baseline, deep enough for the tail of a g. The box's title is the row's label. The headings are left to the book's outline: the cover is its only level-1 heading, each lesson a level-2 one, and the cover style's `sectionEnd: 'heading'` stops its blank running heads at the first lesson.

### 3 · Draw the dots from the face's own outlines

Postext sets a letter in colour, outlined or pale, but it cannot draw it as a row of dots along its stroke. The dots are drawn before the book is set, from the outlines of each Playwrite family's Thin cut: points every 0.2 x-height along each contour, a point kept only when no other lies within 0.8 of that pitch, so the two sides of a thin stroke give a single row. The dot of an i or an accent stays a small filled shape. Each edition's dots, with the copy's and the model's advances, are in `traces-<lang>.json`, and the pen lays the copies along the rows: after the model, across a whole row, one after the model, and an empty row last.

### 4 · Trace characters in their squares

<Excerpt region="squares" />

A Han character or a kana is one em wide, so tracking of the square minus the em sets one character per square, as in a 字帖. The first square of a row holds the character in ink, the next three the same character in the pale red of 描红 (grey in the Japanese edition), and the rest stay empty. A row box lists its four characters as list items, which the design reads one by one with `{boxText.1}` to `{boxText.4}`. A sentence (a `frase` box) takes three rows: written out, traced, then empty, with the punctuation in a square of its own. The Chinese pinyin rows use the four-line ruling of the Latin editions with Andika, whose single-storey a and g are the ones children learn.

### 5 · Mirror nothing for Arabic

The Arabic edition shares every design with the others. A design laid in the flow of a right-to-left page, a heading's or a box's, is mirrored by the engine, so the letter disc, the model and the dictionary's first picture move to the right by themselves; only the dotted copies, drawn inside a picture, are laid from the right edge in code, and the warm-up waves run from right to left. `locale: 'ar'` binds the book on the right.

## Variations

### Use the US cursive

Playwrite has a family for many school models. For American cursive, change the English face and generate its dots again.

```diff
-  en: { hand: 'Playwrite GB J', ruling: 'gb', u: 3.6 },
+  en: { hand: 'Playwrite US Trad', ruling: 'gb', u: 3.6 },
```

### Write on every second Seyès line

Children starting school often write on every other heavy line. Give the French rows a pitch of eight lines.

```diff
-    seyes: { above: 4, below: 2, gap: -2, seyes: true }, // a row on every heavy line
+    seyes: { above: 4, below: 2, gap: 2, seyes: true },
```

## Pitfalls

- A design text clips at its box. A box as tall as one line cuts the descenders of the model's g and p: give it a tall line and place it from the baseline.
- `loadCjkFonts` loads every face for the whole sample: a Chinese face that lacks a character the text uses anywhere fails the load. Name fonts in Latin letters in a colophon.
