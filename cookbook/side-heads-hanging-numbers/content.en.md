# Competition brief {kicker="File BML-2026/04" lead="An open, anonymous competition in two stages to turn the fish market of 1931 into the town’s library." map="Site plan at 1:3000, with the fish market’s plot in red and the built town in grey."}

## The competition

Puerto Lince Town Council is holding a design competition, judged by a jury, to select the architectural proposal for the new Municipal Library, which will occupy the former fish market on the Poniente quay. The present library has operated since 1987 on the ground floor of the Casa de Cultura: 610 m² for a town of 24,300 inhabitants, with no room to grow.

The competition is governed by this brief and by Spain’s Law 9/2017 on Public Sector Contracts. It is open to architects qualified to practise in a Member State of the European Union, entering alone or as the lead of a team.

### Scope of the commission

The winning team will prepare the outline and detailed designs, the health and safety study and the activity licence application, and will supervise the works. The fee, set at €412,000 plus VAT, also covers the design of the fixed furniture and the signage.

### Budget and timescale

The maximum construction budget, which includes the landscaping of the plot, is €4,850,000 excluding VAT. Entries that exceed it by more than 10% will be disqualified. The works will take about twenty months, and the library should open in 2030.

## The site

The fish market stands on the town’s front to the old harbour, between the fishermen’s quarter and the extension of 1905. It is the only building in the old harbour from before 1936 that is still standing, and the Council bought it from the Port Authority in 2022.

### The plot

The plot is a rectangle of 70 by 45 metres, 3,150 m² (:ref{id="parcela" case="lower"}). It is bounded to the south by the Paseo del Muelle, to the east by Calle de la Aduana and to the north by the Plaza de las Redes, which the local plan intends to pedestrianise in 2028. To the west stands the weighbridge hut, owned by the Council, which may be demolished or brought into the scheme.

Outside the hall, 1,300 m² may be built on, in no more than two storeys and 9.5 m to the cornice. No new building may project beyond the line of the quay front.

### The 1931 fish market

The fish market is a reinforced-concrete hall of 52 by 24 metres, roofed by thirteen barrel vaults spanning four metres that rest on portal frames. It was built by the Harbour Works Board, and fish was auctioned in it until 2009, when the auction moved to the new port.

The municipal heritage list protects it at grade 2. The structure, the vaults and the quay front with its eleven round arches must be kept (:ref{id="fachada" case="lower"}). The structural survey of 2025 found corroded reinforcement in the four frames at the east end; their repair is included in the budget, and each entry will explain how it proposes to carry it out without dismantling any of the vaults.

### Access and trees

The main entrance may be from the square or from the promenade, but deliveries will be made from Calle de la Aduana. The six tamarisks along the north edge, some forty years old, are to be kept.

## Schedule of accommodation

The areas in the table are net internal areas (:ref{id="programa" style="full" case="lower"}). Each zone may vary by 10% either way, provided the total does not change. The hall will house the reading and meeting rooms; anything that needs enclosure, services or noise control may go in the new building.

### Public areas

The main reading room will hold the adult lending collection, some 38,000 items, with ninety reader seats. Periodicals and the local history collection will share an adjoining room, with twelve study places and a self-service scanner. The children’s library will have its own entrance from outside and a storytelling corner for twenty-five children. The young people’s room and the multi-purpose hall must be able to open outside library hours, with the rest of the building closed.

### Staff areas

Cataloguing, the offices and the closed stack add up to 260 m². These areas must connect with the loading bay without crossing the public rooms. The stack will have mobile shelving; its floor slab will be designed for an imposed load of 12 kN/m² and will be at street level.

### Outdoor spaces

Between the hall and the new building, an open-air reading courtyard of at least 180 m² is required, sheltered from the north wind, which in winter gusts here at over 60 km/h.

## Design criteria

The jury will assess the entries against the following criteria, listed in descending order of weight.

**The market hall.** Work on the hall must be recognisable as new and reversible. Floors that cut across the vaults will not be accepted; mezzanines that stand clear and leave the frames in full view will.

**Accessibility.** A wheelchair user must reach every part of the building unaided, courtyard and mezzanines included. No ramp may exceed 6%.

**Energy and comfort.** Entries will say how the hall is ventilated and shaded from the sun, since its vaults cannot be insulated on the inside. Credit will be given for heating and cooling controlled room by room.

**Maintenance.** The fish market is 18 metres from the water, and salt spray reaches it all year. New materials will be chosen for those conditions, and the report will include a plan for cleaning roofs and gutters.

**Cost.** The jury will check that the cost estimate keeps within the maximum set in section 1.2.

## Submission of entries

The competition is held in two stages. All documents are to be delivered on paper to the Council’s general registry, and as PDF files to the procurement platform, before 14:00 on the date set.

### Stage one

Each team will submit two A1 boards in portrait format, mounted on lightweight rigid board, showing the general idea, the ground-floor plan at 1:200 and a section through the hall. They will be accompanied by a report of no more than four A4 pages; the jury will not read any pages beyond that limit. Models are not accepted at this stage.

### Stage two

The jury will shortlist five entries, whose authors will develop their idea on three A1 boards, with a ten-page report and a cost estimate broken down by element. Each shortlisted team will receive €6,000 on delivery of the complete documents.

### Anonymity

The boards and the report will carry only a motto of up to five words, repeated on the sealed envelope that holds the team’s details. Any entry bearing names, logos or references by which its authors could be identified will be excluded. The envelopes will be opened at a public session, once the jury has signed its decision.

## Jury and timetable

The jury will be constituted before the deadline for questions, and its membership will be published on the Council’s buyer profile. Its deliberations will be confidential, and the record of the decision will give written reasons for each prize and each commendation.

### Members of the jury

The jury will have seven voting members: the councillor for Culture, who will chair it; the municipal architect; the director of the library; three architects, two of them nominated by the professional association; and a librarian from the regional library service. A procurement officer will act as secretary, with the right to speak but not to vote.

### Prizes

The first prize carries the award of the contract for the design and the supervision of the works. The second prize will receive €12,000 and the third, €8,000. There are also two commendations of €4,000 each, which the jury may decide not to award.

### Timetable

The dates in the table will not change (:ref{id="calendario" style="full" case="lower"}). Questions are to be sent in writing, and the answers will be published without naming whoever asked.
