## What you'll build

Three plates from a school atlas of Varenia, a country invented for this recipe. Plate 1 places it on its continent. Plate 2 spreads the relief map across both pages, from the Lira Sea to the border with Brunavia, in height tints with three rivers and the line of a section. Plate 3 draws that section through the Albar Range. Each plate opens on a blue band and ends with a key or a glossary and a few activities for the class. The three maps are paintings made with an image model and carry no letter; borders, towns, the scale and the north arrow are vector symbols drawn with them. The line of section is no part of the picture: two connectors of the template join two anchors of the map. Every name, height and scale figure is a `::label` line in the Markdown, set at anchors stored with each map, and so is the key of plate 1, a legend that stands in the sea. The Spanish, English and Arabic editions share the same three paintings, and the Arabic one reads and binds from the right.

## Method

### 1 · A place is a point of the drawing

<Excerpt region="anchors" />

An anchor is a point in fractions of the picture's width and height, so it holds at any size the frame sets the map, and when the reflow ladder sets a map smaller its names go with it. `km()` turns the kilometres read off a grid laid over the painting into those fractions: a town's dot and its name both come from `TOWNS` and cannot drift apart. The anchor holds what belongs to the place, not to the language: its style, the turn of a river's or a range's name along its course, and the side of the name that stands at the point. Town names stand centred above their dot, because `align: 'start'` would be the left of an English name and the right of an Arabic one.

The paintings were made in order. The relief plate came first, painted by an image model from a hill-shaded height map of the country; the locator map was painted from the relief plate and the section from the same heights, so coasts and heights agree on all three. A grid drawn over each picture gave the kilometres of every anchor, and the borders were cut where they reach the painted coast.

### 2 · Three Markdown files, one resource

<Excerpt region="resources" />

The resource has one picture (an SVG that holds the painting and the vector symbols over it) and one list of anchors for every edition. Each edition writes one `::label[…]{at="…"}` line per anchor inside `:::figure{resource="relief" slot="map"}`, so `::label[Calvera Plain]{at="plain"}` and `::label[Llanura de Calvera]{at="plain"}` stand on the same spot, broken into two lines by the anchor's `width` when they need it. The alternative text changes with the edition too: each `:::figure` carries its own in a `::alt[…]` line, and a caption or a note would go the same way with `::caption[…]` and `::note[…]`. An anchor with a style that an edition leaves without a label is reported as `anchorUnlabelled`, and a label whose `at` names no anchor is not drawn and is reported as `labelAnchorUnknown`.

### 3 · A sheet and a double sheet

<Excerpt region="templates" />

The sheet and the double sheet have the same five frames, so a plate's Markdown has the same shape on one page or on two: the title, the text that names no slot, the map, the key and the activities. The locator sheet `extends` the sheet and takes the key frame out (`remove: true`), since its key is a label on the map; its activities hang under the map. The line of section belongs to the double sheet: two `connector` elements of its decoration run `straight` from the anchor `a-end` to the anchor `b-end` of the picture the map frame holds, a 0.9 mm paper line under a 0.5 mm dashed ink line with a dot at each end. A connector's end with `anchor` follows the picture through its crop and its scale, so the line stays on A and B if the ladder sets the map smaller. Decoration paints under the frames, but a connector with an `anchor` end points into a picture, so it is painted over them and the line shows on the painting. On the double sheet `spread: true` lays both pages as one sheet 390 mm wide, and only the map frame sets `crossGutter`, so it runs over the spine while the text and the boxes keep to their own pages. The text, the map and the key form a `stack` group: in the bench's stress test, with every text 30 % longer, the Arabic edition sets the locator map at 87.5 % and the boxes under it move up, with no warning.

### 4 · A key on the map, and names with leaders

<Excerpt region="answer" />

The `legend` style is a label with `kind: 'legend'`, a white plate, a hairline border and round corners. Its Markdown is a `:::label{at="key" style="legend"}` block inside the figure, a row per line: `:icon[]{resource="key-mark" color="country"} Varenia` tints one small square resource with a palette colour, `:icon[]{line="dashed" color="border"}` draws a sample of the border, and the symbols that open the rows hang in one column, so the words line up on the right of it, or on its left in Arabic. The brackets are empty: the word that follows names the row, and a symbol with no alternative text is read as decoration. On the section, the anchors `sela` and `plain` stand on the ground and their `labelAt` puts the names 850 m up in the sky; the `region` style's `leader` draws a 0.6 pt line with a dot from each name down to its point.

### 5 · One palette for the page, the tints and the key

<Excerpt region="palette" />

The five height tints are palette entries (`palette.low` to `palette.peak`), set to the median colour of the painted relief in each height band, and the key of plate 2 writes `:swatch{color="low"}` with the same ids. The painting does not read the palette: a new hex changes the key and the vector symbols and leaves the picture as it is, so to change the map's colours you paint it again. Water names in `water` have a contrast of at least 6:1 on the painted sea, and the white title has 7.2:1 on `band`.

### 6 · The Arabic edition

The code is [the short answer](#the-short-answer) above. `locale: 'ar'` sets the text right to left and binds the book on the right, so the spread opens on the right-hand page and the frames follow the mirrored content area: the title and the text stand on the right, the key to the right of the activities. The maps are not mirrored, and each Arabic name stands where its place is drawn. The label styles lose their italics and letter-spacing in Arabic and grow by 15 %, since Kufi sets small. The decoration and the folios stay on the sheet, so the kicker moves to the top right and the folios swap sides.

## Variations

### Name a place the map has no anchor for

A label can stand at a point given in percentages of the picture, with a style of its own.

```diff
 :::figure{resource="relief" slot="map"}
+::label[Nema Light]{at="7% 68%" style="town"}
 ::label[Misty Ocean]{at="ocean"}
```

### Number the maps and caption them

Give the map type a counter and a prefix, and each resource a caption: see <RecipeLink slug="atlas-map-sheet">the atlas sheet with numbered maps</RecipeLink>, which captions its maps on a blue bar.

## Pitfalls

- The alternative text of each map lives in the Markdown, as a `::alt[…]` line inside its `:::figure`, one per edition. A figure that gives none keeps the resource's own `altText`; `script.js` holds no word of the page.
- A connector whose ends name only frames is painted under them, with the rest of the decoration; only an end with `anchor` lifts it over the picture. A line from a note to the map's edge, with no anchor, is hidden where it crosses the painting.
- The anchors and the vector symbols (borders, towns, peaks, the ends of the line of section) stand at kilometres read off the painting. Paint a map again and its coasts, rivers and ridges move: lay the grid over the new picture and read them again, or a name will land in the sea.
- On a right-to-left page the frames are mirrored with the content area, but the template's decoration is not: place a kicker by edition, as `kicker` does.
- A frame in two columns may cut a paragraph one line before its end and leave that line at the head of the second column. Read the columns of each edition and move a sentence from one paragraph to the next when that happens.
