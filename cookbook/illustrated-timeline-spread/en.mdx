## What you'll build

Pages 48 and 49 of a lower-secondary history book: *The paper road*, a thousand years of papermaking from Cai Lun's workshop to Keller's grindstone. The left page opens with the title and two paragraphs, the right page with a box on how a sheet was made by hand. Across the middle runs the timeline: an indigo band for Asia, a terracotta band for Europe that starts on the left page and crosses the spine. Under it, six cards with a picture each, hung from the band by a dot and a drop. The template draws one Asian card, one European card and one connector per band: a grid lays a card for every date the Markdown gives, and the connector finds every card the grid laid. Every word is in the Markdown, in Spanish, English and Japanese.

## Method

### 1 · One sheet for two pages

<Excerpt region="answer" />

`spread: true` makes the template one sheet as wide as both pages, and the page fence says the same with `spread`. A spread opens on a verso, so the build passes `continuation: { pageIndexOffset: 1, pageNumbering: { startAt: 48 } }`: page 1 of this excerpt is the book's page 48, a left page, and no blank page comes first. The grid has one column per page, with a gap of twice the inner margin, so nothing but the band comes near the fold. The second band sets `crossGutter` and a left padding of 36 mm: its colour runs from the end of the first band across the spine, and its words start on the right page, where the layout reads them.

### 2 · One card, repeated by a grid

<Excerpt region="cards" />

A group of `kind: 'grid'` with `repeat: { frames: ['asia'] }` lays the `asia` frame once for every `:::frame{slot="asia"}` of the page, three to a row, in the template grid's cell `col: 1`, rows 6 to 12. The copies are named `#asia[1]`, `#asia[2]` and `#asia[3]`, and each takes its own fence's `label`, the date on its tab. A card is at least `minHeight` tall and `auto` above that: when one text runs long, as the 1844 card does in Spanish and Japanese, its row grows and the other cards of the row stretch to the same height (`rowAlign` is `'stretch'` by default). A fourth date in the Markdown starts a second row under the first; the template does not change.

### 3 · A connector for every band

<Excerpt region="ticks" />

The ticks are one `connector` per band in the template's `decoration`. `routing: 'tree'` joins one parent to many children; with `trunk: 0` it draws no bar of its own, only a straight drop from the band's foot to the middle of each card's top, and `startCap: 'dot'` puts the dot at the head of each drop, 2.4 mm under the band (`gap`). `#asia[*]` names every card the grid laid on the page, so a date added or removed in the Markdown adds or removes its drop. Connectors are laid after the frames, so when a row grows the drops still meet the cards. The cream ground is decoration too, a box anchored to the bleed's foot whose height runs up to the foot of `#band-1` (`size: { height: { to: '#band-1', edge: 'bottom' } }`), so it follows the band.

### 4 · Pictures at the size they print

<Excerpt region="art" />

The vignettes are 1,000 px wide at 550 ppi, 46 mm on the page, and a picture is never enlarged. The grid's three equal columns are 56.3 mm wide on each page, so each picture sits inside its card's 3.5 mm padding at its own size and the rest of the card is text.

## Variations

### Four cards to a row

With `columns: 4` each band takes four dates side by side; the cards are narrower and their texts run longer, so the rows grow taller.

```diff
+const row = (name, col) => ({ name: `${name}-row`, kind: 'grid', columns: 4, columnGap: mm(5),
```

### Arrows instead of dots

`endCap: 'arrow'` puts an arrowhead where each drop meets its card, and `startCap: 'none'` takes the dots away.

## Pitfalls

- A grid lays one cell per item, and an item is a `:::frame{slot="asia"}` fence of its own: two cards' texts inside one fence make one card.
- `#asia` without `[*]` is the grid's box, not its cards: a connector to it draws one drop to the middle of the row.
- A size that runs to an edge of another box counts from the element's own anchor point: the ground is anchored to the bleed's foot and grows up to `#band-1`. An edge behind the anchor gives a height of 0.
