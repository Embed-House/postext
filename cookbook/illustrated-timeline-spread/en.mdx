## What you'll build

Pages 48 and 49 of a lower-secondary history book: *The paper road*, a thousand years of papermaking from Cai Lun's workshop to Keller's grindstone. The left page opens with the title and two paragraphs, the right page with a box on how a sheet was made by hand. Across the middle runs the timeline: an indigo band for Asia, a terracotta band for Europe that starts on the left page and crosses the spine. Under it, six cards with a picture each, hung from the band by a dot and a tick. The cards share their width: the 1844 card, with the longest story, takes width from its two neighbours (1 mm in Spanish, 7 mm in Japanese), and its dot moves with it. Every word is in the Markdown, in Spanish, English and Japanese.

## Method

### 1 · One sheet for two pages

<Excerpt region="answer" />

`spread: true` makes the template one sheet as wide as both pages, and the page fence says the same with `spread`. A spread opens on a verso, so the build passes `continuation: { pageIndexOffset: 1, pageNumbering: { startAt: 48 } }`: page 1 of this excerpt is the book's page 48, a left page, and no blank page comes first. The grid has one column per page, with a gap of twice the inner margin, so nothing but the band comes near the fold. The second band sets `crossGutter` and a left padding of 36 mm: its colour runs from the end of the first band across the spine, and its words start on the right page, where the layout reads them.

### 2 · Cards that trade width

<Excerpt region="cards" />

Each row of three cards is a `row` group, and each card has a `minHeight`, the height it is drawn at, and an `auto` height above that. When a card's text runs past the row's height, it takes the fewest whole millimetres it needs from the neighbours with `shrink`, each of which keeps the width its own text still fits in. Only when they have nothing more to give does the row grow taller. In the Spanish edition the 1844 card takes 1 mm from 1282; in Japanese it takes 7 mm, 3 from 1150 and 4 from 1282; with every text 30 % longer, the bench test, the Spanish card takes 15 mm and the box and the lead set their text at 92.5 % and 85 %.

### 3 · Dots that follow the cards

<Excerpt region="marks" />

The dots and ticks are the template's `decoration`, anchored to `#c1` … `#c6` at their `top` and raised by the distance to the band. Decoration is laid after the frames, so when a card moves or widens, its dot stays over its centre. The cream ground is decoration too, a box anchored to the bleed's foot whose height runs up to the foot of `#band-1` (`size: { height: { to: '#band-1', edge: 'bottom' } }`), so it follows the band.

### 4 · Pictures at the size they print

<Excerpt region="art" />

The vignettes are 1,000 px wide at 550 ppi, 46 mm on the page, and a picture is never enlarged: a card that widens keeps its picture and gives the room to its text. A card narrower than 53 mm (the picture plus 3.5 mm of padding on each side) sets its picture smaller, which the stress test shows in the Japanese edition: the 1150 and 1282 pictures at 85 %.

## Variations

### Keep the cards the same width

With `shrink: 0` no card gives width: the row grows taller instead, all three cards together, and its foot moves down the page.

```diff
-  shrink: 1, // a card gives width to a neighbour whose text runs past the row
+  shrink: 0,
```

## Pitfalls

- A card with a fixed `height` takes no part in the width trade, since it never runs past the row: give the cards a `minHeight`, as here, and leave the height `auto`.
- A size that runs to an edge of another box counts from the element's own anchor point: the ground is anchored to the bleed's foot and grows up to `#band-1`. An edge behind the anchor gives a height of 0.
