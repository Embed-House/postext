## What you'll build

A performing edition of the two menuets from the 1725 Notebook for Anna Magdalena Bach, the G major one every piano pupil learns and its G minor companion, now given to Christian Petzold. The page is 230 × 305 mm, the size of a sheet-music album. A recto introduces the pieces in two columns of Libre Caslon under a 52 pt Bodoni title. Then each menuet opens on a verso and runs across the spread in a grand staff of 7 mm, set in Bravura from a MusicXML file: four bars to a system, bars 1–16 on the left page and 17–32 on the right, so the player turns no page inside a piece.

## Method

### 1 · Bring the piece in as MusicXML

The two files in `assets/` are plain MusicXML 4.0, the format Dorico, Sibelius, Finale and MuseScore write when you export a score (choose the uncompressed `.musicxml`, not `.mxl`). They hold one part on two staves, with the clefs, key, beams, stem directions of the two-voice bars, ornaments and repeat barlines. The part name is marked `print-object="no"`: a printed "Piano" label would push every system to the right. The title in `<work-title>` is not printed, only read as the score's description, so the Markdown gives each piece a heading of its own.

### 2 · Set it in a fence, not a resource

The [short answer](#the-short-answer) fetches each file and writes it into a ` ```musicxml ` fence, where the Markdown keeps a placeholder line. A fence is engraved at the width of the column and cut into systems, and each system is placed like a paragraph line, so the piece goes on to the next page between two systems. A `score` resource would be kept whole and could not cross a page. `minSystemsAtBreak: 2` keeps a single system from being left alone at the foot of a page or carried to the head of the next.

### 3 · Size the staff for the player

The staff is 7 mm high, the size of a printed piano album read from the music desk. `spacing: 1.7` widens the notes until a system of the 186 mm measure holds four bars, so each half of a menuet takes four systems. `systemGap: 14 mm` leaves room for the ledger lines between the hands, and with it four systems fill the opening page under the title and four the facing one: the double bar falls at the turn of the spread.

### 4 · Open every piece on a verso

<Excerpt region="openers" />

`breakBefore: { parity: 'even' }` starts each piece on a left-hand page, and the editor's title page uses a heading style that opens on a recto with no running heads. The catalogue number and the composer come from the heading's attributes and sit at the bottom corners of the opener, where engraved editions print them.

### 5 · Name the piece at the head of the right page

<Excerpt region="running-heads" />

The opening page of a piece carries its title, so the running heads are kept to the pages after it (`pages: 'body'`). The recto prints the piece's title and the folio at the outer edge, in the label face at 8 pt.

## Variations

### Set a study edition on a smaller staff

A 6 mm staff at the engraver's own spacing holds seven or eight bars to a system, so each menuet fits on its opening page.

```diff
-  staffSize: mm(7),
+  staffSize: mm(6),
-  spacing: 1.7,
+  spacing: 1,
```

### Number short examples instead

When the music is a set of short examples cited in the text, a numbered `score` resource is the better fit: <RecipeLink slug="conservatory-method-chorales">four-part chorales as numbered examples</RecipeLink>.

## Pitfalls

- System breaks written in the file (`<print new-system="yes">`) are not kept: the systems fill the width of the column, so tune `spacing` and `staffSize` until they hold the bars you want.
- The engraver does not even out the bars between systems, and the last system is set at its natural width: at 6 mm and `spacing: 1`, bar 32 stands alone on a system of its own. Change `spacing` until the last system holds more than a bar.
- A compressed `.mxl` file is a zip archive: unzip it, or export the uncompressed format, before you fetch it.
